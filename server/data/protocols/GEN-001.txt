GEN-001 General Medical and Respiratory Triage Protocol

Use this protocol for fever, cough, fatigue, dizziness, weakness, nonspecific illness, shortness of breath without a clearer specialty pattern, or other general medical complaints. Assess severity from the patient’s wording and supplied profile, not from assumptions. Severe breathlessness, cyanosis, inability to speak normally, fainting, confusion, signs of shock, rapidly worsening symptoms, or inability to maintain hydration are emergency warning features. Respiratory history such as asthma or chronic lung disease, advanced age, immune compromise, pregnancy, and significant medication use can increase risk. A mild stable complaint without warning features may be lower acuity, while persistent or worsening symptoms need timely clinical assessment. Do not claim that an infection, pneumonia, asthma attack, or other diagnosis is confirmed unless the input explicitly states it as established history. Identify relevant allergies, chronic conditions, adherence concerns, and medication-related risks as modifiers. Use special flags only when supported by the patient profile or symptom text. The model must not hallucinate vital signs, examination findings, tests, treatment, facility names, or routing decisions. Return only the required structured prediction and evidence summary, with each evidence item tied to supplied information or clearly marked as unavailable.
