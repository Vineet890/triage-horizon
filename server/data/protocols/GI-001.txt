GI-001 Gastrointestinal Symptoms Triage Protocol

Use this protocol for abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, gastrointestinal bleeding, swallowing difficulty, or other digestive complaints. Emergency warning features include severe or rapidly worsening abdominal pain, rigid or distended abdomen, fainting, confusion, persistent inability to keep fluids down, blood in vomit or stool, black stool, signs of shock, or severe dehydration. Pregnancy, anticoagulant therapy, older age, prior abdominal surgery, inflammatory bowel disease, liver disease, and significant comorbidities may increase concern. The model should use only the described symptoms and supplied profile; it must not infer examination findings, laboratory results, pregnancy status, or a specific diagnosis. Mild, brief symptoms without warning features may be lower acuity, while ongoing symptoms, dehydration risk, or significant comorbidity warrant prompt clinical review. Risk modifiers should include documented allergies, medications, chronic disease, and adherence information when relevant. Special flags must be grounded in explicit evidence, such as anticoagulant therapy or reported bleeding. Do not prescribe, recommend a named facility, or make a routing decision. The output is limited to protocol code, acuity, risk modifiers, special flags, and a short evidence summary. State uncertainty when key severity information is absent.
