NEURO-001 Acute Neurological Symptoms Triage Protocol

Use this protocol for sudden weakness or numbness, facial droop, speech difficulty, confusion, seizure, severe new headache, loss of coordination, or other potentially acute neurological symptoms. Sudden onset, one-sided deficits, altered consciousness, seizure activity, inability to walk, severe unexplained headache, neck stiffness, or symptoms after significant trauma are emergency warning features. Establish the reported time of onset or last known well when available, but never delay escalation while seeking a complete history. Relevant modifiers include previous stroke, atrial fibrillation, anticoagulant therapy, hypertension, diabetes, seizure history, pregnancy, recent infection, and head injury. The model must distinguish reported facts from uncertain or absent information. It must not diagnose stroke, hemorrhage, seizure disorder, or migraine solely from this protocol. Persistent or progressive neurological symptoms require urgent clinical evaluation. Stable, mild, recurrent symptoms without warning features still require clinician review and clear instructions to seek emergency care if symptoms change. Do not select a hospital, specialist, transport method, or treatment. Predict only protocol, acuity, risk modifiers, special flags, and concise evidence based on the supplied symptom and profile. Avoid unsupported claims and do not infer a normal neurological examination.
